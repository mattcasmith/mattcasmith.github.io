Cosplaying as a founder at the Corgi Café in London

2026-08-15  Technology,   Productivity

One of my most-read blog posts is from last October, when Anthropic put on a pop-up café in New York that indirectly convinced me to switch to Claude as my regular AI model. Perhaps the marketers were so excited that their “activation” had indeed activated somebody that they couldn’t stop reading. Either way, I realise that by returning to the tech-adjacent café scene there’s some risk of my blog becoming a specialist publication by accident.

This time it’s the turn of Corgi – a business insurance startup. When I saw a tweet promoting its new Corgi Café in London, I was intrigued – less about the café itself, and more about what it represented for the city.

Although the so-called Silicon Roundabout is still physically there, it’s safe to say London never achieved the entrepreneurial vibe of somewhere like Austin or San Francisco. Would a 24-hour, laptop-friendly, grindset café provide a much-needed slice of startup vibrancy in an otherwise very grey city? One morning I took a detour from my regular commute to find out.

Arriving on the scene

I felt something of an impostor as I spotted the orange of the Corgi Café on Great Eastern Street. The day before, I’d seen an X post showing east London types spilling out onto the pavement outside – and on a Saturday night, no less! Here I was, about to cosplay as a founder on my way to my corporate job, taking up precious space that could be used to build the future.

I shouldn’t have worried. If there are indeed builders in London, they’re not early risers. When I arrived, there were two other people sitting at tables and working on their laptops, and one of those turned out to be an employee who eventually migrated behind the counter. A third actual customer joined us after a little while, but it was hardly a buzzing who’s who of the tech scene.

The space itself is bare bones. It has a warehouse-esque aesthetic – exposed wooden floors, barely-painted brick walls, and benches dipped in orange. The ceiling sports a mural inspired by The Creation of Adam, with corgis in place of Adam and God. The menu above the counter reads: “Coffee for building things. Tea for pretending you are taking a break.” All very Shoreditch.

The coffee was good, the WiFi was passable, and the pastries were out of stock. Prices bordered on shocking for a startup-backed café – I paid £4.10 for a cappuccino that was admittedly very good, but that’s still way above the going rate even for London, and definitely for a coffee served in a paper cup. I guess that’s what you pay for 24/7 opening hours when it gets this quiet.

A new third space

Did my visit to the Corgi Café inspire an entrepreneurial epiphany? Will I be quitting my job and taking my chances as a founder? Not any time soon.

I’ll admit I was a little disappointed not to feel more of a hustle culture vibe after the level of hype I’d seen. But to be fair, I might have predicted that the café would be more of an evening place than a morning one. Founders are famous for pulling all-nighters to smash out code, but the reality is that they are still actual people who need actual sleep, and I suppose those marathons come at the expense of the morning hours when the body needs to catch up.

While I’ll have to go back and visit in the evening or on a weekend to get the full Corgi Café experience, the place does provide something London is sorely missing: a third space open into the night that doesn’t centre on drinking. All the coffee shops in the City are closed by the time I leave the office, so having somewhere nearby to sit and focus on writing or coding is valuable.

While I doubt the Corgi Café will meet its online hype as the saviour of the London tech scene, I do hope it will be an influence on our nighttime culture. But if it is bursting at the seams with founders and developers in the later hours, I’ll still be happy to drop by for the occasional quiet morning coffee.
